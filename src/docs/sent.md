**"Sentinel"** primarily means **a guard, watcher, or protector** — someone (or something) that stands watch, keeps vigil, and defends against threats or surprises.

### Core Meanings
- **Literal / Military**: A soldier or sentry posted to guard a position, challenge intruders, and prevent surprise attacks. It's an older, somewhat literary term for a lookout or watchman.
- **Figurative / General**: Any person, animal, or thing that watches over or protects something. Examples:
  - "The cats were the sentinels of the house" (constantly patrolling for threats).
  - Tall trees "standing sentinel" over a landscape.
- **Etymology**: From French *sentinelle* (16th century), from Italian *sentinella*, likely rooted in Latin *sentire* ("to feel, perceive, sense") — emphasizing vigilant awareness.

Synonyms include: **guard**, **sentry**, **lookout**, **watchman**, **guardian**, **custodian**.

### In Computing & Technology (Very Relevant to Your Project)
- A **sentinel** often refers to a monitoring or protective mechanism:
  - **Sentinel value** — a special marker in data (e.g., a unique value signaling the end of a list or a missing entry).
  - In security: Tools or systems that act as watchful guardians (e.g., Microsoft Sentinel — a cloud-native SIEM for threat detection, investigation, and response).
  - In modern AI/agent systems: **Sentinel agents** or **sentinel modules** are oversight components that monitor other agents or environments for anomalies, risks, unsafe actions, or policy violations. They observe, detect, and sometimes intervene or alert — without necessarily executing the main work.
