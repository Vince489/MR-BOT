Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs), which are trained on massive text data, have demonstrated remarkable advancements in language understanding capabilities. Nevertheless, it remains unclear to what extent LLMs have effectively captured and utilized the implicit relationships inherent in the text. This study introduces ‘Understanding Self-Consistency’, a new perspective that reflects LLMs’ ability to grasp in-depth knowledge relationships through their consistency performance. Specifically, Understanding Self-Consistency refers to the model’s capacity to maintain logical and contextual consistency between inputs and responses. Inspired by human cognitive behavior, we design a self-check action framework named 
. Wherein, a self-question and answering mechanism is emphasized and forms a logically closed loop including four classes of actions, allowing our

Experimental results on six LLMs across two datasets show that LLMs exhibit objective ability values of the understanding self-consistency and demonstrate their differentiated grasp of knowledge relationships across different reasoning paradigms. Moreover, our findings reveal that LLMs’ performance can be improved with their own outputs (which we call ‘self-enhanced Feedforward’). Notably,

Introduction
Transformer (Liu et al., 2024, Vaswani et al., 2017) -based large language models (LLMs), such as GPT-4 (Achiam et al., 2023), LLaMA (Touvron et al., 2023), and ERNIE (ERNIE, 2023), which are trained on massive text data (Minaee et al., 2024), have demonstrated remarkable advancements in language understanding capabilities (Zhu, Li, Liu, & Zhao, 2024). This ability is widely regarded as a fundamental aspect that contributes to the emergence of LLM intelligence, highlighted in various linguistic tasks, including machine translation (Zhao, Zhang, & Zong, 2023), sentiment analysis (Wang, Guo et al., 2023), text summarization (Glazkova & Morozov, 2023), and question-answering systems (Nassiri & Akhloufi, 2023). However, despite these impressive capabilities, an important question remains: to what extent have these LLMs effectively captured and utilized the implicit semantic and logical relationships inherent in the text they have learned? Specifically, the degree to which these models “grasp” knowledge — i.e., their ability to understand and represent the information encoded in the data — remains unclear. This abstract issue is critical, particularly in real-world application scenarios (Mohammed and Aljanabi, 2024, Nabi et al., 2023), where logical reasoning and consistency are paramount.
From the linguistic logic perspective, self-consistency is defined as the invariance of an LLM’s responses across semantics-preserving prompt transformations (Elazar et al., 2021). In particular, this property is intrinsically linked with understanding and grasping knowledge, forming a mutually reinforcing relationship where improvements in one drive advancements in the other. Specifically, when the responses generated by an LLM are consistent with the knowledge it has learned, it demonstrates the model’s ability to grasp the relevant knowledge relationships. Conversely, a deeper grasp of knowledge relationships enables the model to maintain higher levels of self-consistency, thereby facilitating more accurate reasoning.
Existing studies on logical self-consistency (Chen, Aksitov et al., 2024, Chen, Zhang, Nguyen et al., 2023, Cheng, Zouhar et al., 2024, Gou et al., 2024, Li et al., 2022, Shinn et al., 2024, Wang, Wei et al., 2023, Yao et al., 2023) have made significant strides, however, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a), they predominantly focus on the output level that examines whether multiple responses generated for the same input are consistent. These methods often overlook the deeper semantic and logical relationships embedded in the knowledge being evaluated. Consequently, outputs that appear self-consistent may not align with the actual semantic and logical structure of the input, which poses significant risks in critical real-world applications such as medical diagnostics, legal advice, and financial reporting (Tonmoy et al., 2024).
To address these gaps, this paper introduces a new perspective, ‘understanding self-consistency’, to emphasize LLMs’ ability to grasp knowledge relationships inherent in the text they have learned. Unlike existing notions of self-consistency, as shown in Fig. 1(b), ‘understanding self-consistency’ focuses on the internal consistency between LLMs’ inputs and responses, reflecting their ability to capture and leverage the implicit logic encoded in the text, i.e. the degree of grasp for knowledge in text. Consequently, we propose a novel action framework 
 to evaluate the deeper understanding of LLM. Wherein, a self-question and answering mechanism inspired by human cognitive behavior is explored as a self-check strategy, forming a logically closed loop that includes four classes of actions: Generator, Proposer, Responder, and Estimator. Specifically, for a given input, after preprocessing, which involves establishing entity-relation entries and randomly selecting a corresponding preset_answers from the input, the Generator first generates descriptive text by integrating relevant knowledge; next, the Proposer, using this generated text, creates logical questions by leveraging text understanding and creative thinking; then, the Responder provides answers to these logical questions based on its understanding; finally, the Estimator evaluates whether the answers to the questions are semantically consistent with the initially preset_answers from the dataset. Fig. 2 further exemplifies this method. In this context, self-check refers to the process by which the LLM validates the consistency of its own generation, response, and evaluation autonomously.
To evaluate the LLMs’ performance more realistically, we introduce the LogicAware dataset, which maps physical reality and encompass a broad range of factual logical relationships across 14 categories. Designed not to replace but to complement existing resources, LogicAware focuses on knowledge relationships in the real world, and is expected to provide a testing and optimization environment for our method. Certainly, our method can also be extended to other authoritative datasets such as FOLIO (Han et al., 2022), allowing for cross-validation and strengthening the generalization of our approach across different logical reasoning tasks. For both datasets, we design three different reasoning paradigms: Choice Questions (CQs), Black-Fill Questions (BFQs), and Relational Reasoning Questions (LRQs). Experimental analysis on six LLMs (GPT-4 (Achiam et al., 2023), GPT-3.5 (OpenAI, 2022), ERNIE-4 (ERNIE, 2023), SPARK-3 (IFLYTEK, 2023), LLaMA3-8B and LLaMA-70B (Dubey et al., 2024)) shows that our approach effectively quantifies the degree of knowledge grasp of LLMs by measuring their understanding self-consistency. This evaluation objectively reflects the extent to which different LLMs capture and utilize the knowledge relationships across various reasoning paradigms.
In addition, in the experimental design, we strategically introduce a self-enhanced feedforward (SEF) mechanism, the core of which is to utilize the previous response to enrich and refine the subsequent reasoning stages. Specifically, during the Generator phase, the model generates descriptive text, which is then used as contextual background in the Responder phase. This process enhances the relevance and depth of the reasoning, acting as a static enhancement that strengthens the model’s understanding. Quantitative results show that SEF significantly improves the understanding self-consistency of LLMs, highlighting its potential for optimizing knowledge grasp. These findings provide a practical strategy to advance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs and enhance their application in complex tasks.
In summary, our key contributions can be summarized as follows1:
•
We innovatively concretize the LLM’s grasp of knowledge relationships into a quantitative evaluation of the ‘understanding self-consistency’, reflecting their grasp of knowledge relationships from a deeper semantic and logical perspective.
•
We propose an action framework 
 for self-checking the understanding self-consistency through a self-question and answering mechanism. It forms a logically closed loop consisting of four classes of actions, allowing the LLM to generate, question, answer, and evaluate autonomously.
•
Experiments of six LLMs on two datasets, LogicAware and FOLIO, demonstrate that our approach effectively quantifies the degree of knowledge grasp of LLMs by evaluating their understanding self-consistency. The results further reveal that different LLMs exhibit distinct performances in understanding self-consistency across various reasoning paradigms.
•
We also demonstrate that the performance of LLMs can be improved effectively with their own outputs by our proposed Self-Enhanced Feedforward (SEF) mechanism.
